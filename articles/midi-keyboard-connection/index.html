Connecting a MIDI keyboard on PC, Mac, iPhone and Android

USB, 5 pin or Bluetooth, and the traps on each system.

Plugging a keyboard into a computer should be simple. Sometimes it is. Sometimes you spend 40 minutes wondering why nothing shows up. Here is everything I learned about connecting a MIDI keyboard to a browser app like this one, depending on what you are on.

First, the browser

Web apps talk to your keyboard through something called Web MIDI. Not every browser has it.

  • Chrome, Edge, Opera, Brave on Windows, Mac, Linux and Android: yes.
  • Firefox on desktop: yes, it asks for a permission the first time.
  • Safari on Mac: no.
  • Any browser on iPhone or iPad: no. More on that below.

The first time you turn MIDI on, the browser asks if the site can use your MIDI devices. Say yes. If you said no by accident, click the icon next to the address bar and reset the permission.

The three ways to connect

USB. Most keyboards from the last 15 years have a square USB port on the back, the same as on a printer. One USB cable to your computer and you are done. No driver needed for almost all of them, they are "class compliant".

5 pin MIDI. Older keyboards and stage pianos have round DIN sockets labelled MIDI IN and MIDI OUT. You need a USB MIDI interface. Plug its IN cable into the keyboard's MIDI OUT (yes, out to in), and the USB end into the computer.

Bluetooth. Some recent keyboards have Bluetooth MIDI built in. For the others, small adapters plug into the MIDI or USB port and make it wireless. Handy, but it is also where most of the headaches are, see below.

If you can, use a cable. Less latency, nothing to pair, it just works.

Windows

USB works out of the box. Two things to know:

One app at a time. On Windows a MIDI device can usually only be opened by one program. If your DAW, Synthesia or another tab already has the keyboard, the browser will not see it, or will see it and get nothing. Close the other app and reload the page.

Bluetooth MIDI is a pain. Windows can pair a Bluetooth MIDI keyboard, but browsers do not see it through the classic Windows MIDI system. The workaround is a bridge: a small app like MIDIberry receives the Bluetooth MIDI and sends it to a virtual port created with loopMIDI, and the browser listens to that port. It works but it is fiddly. A cable is way easier. Microsoft is rolling out a new MIDI system for Windows 11 that should fix this, I would not count on it yet.

Mac

USB works out of the box. Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox, not Safari.

For Bluetooth, do not pair in the normal Bluetooth settings. Open Audio MIDI Setup (in Applications, Utilities), then Window, Show MIDI Studio, then click the Bluetooth icon. Your keyboard shows up there, click Connect. After that the browser sees it like a cable.

iPhone and iPad

Bad news: no browser on iOS supports Web MIDI. Chrome and Firefox on iPhone are Safari underneath (Apple requires it), so they have the same limits. Cable or Bluetooth, it does not matter, the web page cannot see the keyboard.

What you can do:

  • Use the microphone mode of the trainer instead. It listens to your piano through the phone's mic. Less precise than MIDI but it works fine for chords.
  • Use one of the small App Store browsers that add Web MIDI support (search "Web MIDI browser"). They open the site inside their own view and pass MIDI through.

For native iOS apps, MIDI over USB works with a USB C cable on recent iPhones, or Apple's Lightning to USB Camera Adapter on older ones.

Android

Chrome on Android supports Web MIDI, so this one works.

USB. You need an OTG cable or adapter: USB C (or micro USB) on the phone side, USB B on the keyboard side. If nothing happens, pull down the notifications. There is often a "USB" notification where you can switch from "Charging only" to "MIDI". Some keyboards also need their own power supply because the phone cannot power them.

Bluetooth. Android does not show Bluetooth MIDI devices in the normal Bluetooth settings, you will not find your keyboard there. Pair it first with a free app like "MIDI BLE Connect", then open Chrome. Once paired, the browser sees it.

Still nothing? Checklist

  1. Is the site on https? Web MIDI only works on secure pages.
  2. Did you allow MIDI in the browser prompt?
  3. Is another app using the keyboard? (Windows especially.)
  4. Is your USB cable a data cable? Some cables only charge. Try another one.
  5. Is the keyboard on the right mode? Some keyboards have a USB mode or a Bluetooth mode setting.
  6. Reload the page after plugging in. The trainer picks up new devices on the fly, but a reload never hurts.

Once it works, it keeps working. It is the first connection that is annoying.

Once your keyboard is connected, turn on MIDI in the trainer and play the chord on screen. It checks the notes you hold, inversions included.
